Passage 1    The Long March Spirit（长征精神：当代青年的必修课）

【阅读原文】

     The Long March Spirit: A Lesson for Today’s Youth

     Every September, China holds National Defense Education Month. This year, the theme is
“Carrying Forward the Spirit of the Long March, Building a Strong National Defense Together.”
But what does a historic journey from 90 years ago have to do with today’s young people? The
answer, educators say, is everything.

     The Long March was a 12,500-kilometer journey made by the Red Army through mountains,
rivers, and frozen lands. Soldiers faced hunger, illness, and constant battles. Many lost their lives,
but those who survived never gave up. Their story has become the “Long March Spirit” — a
symbol of courage, unity, and never giving up in the face of hardship.

     At this year’s opening ceremony in Beijing, students did not just listen to speeches. They
visited exhibitions of military equipment. They took part in simulation training, trying simple
military drills. They also heard stories from old soldiers about what it truly means to protect one’s
country. A student named Wang Lei said, “I used to think national defense was only for soldiers.
Now I understand it is everyone’s responsibility.”

     Why does this matter for students today? A teacher at the event explained, “We live in a
peaceful time, but peace does not come easily. It is built and protected by those who are willing to
sacrifice. When young people learn about the Long March, they learn to value what they have and
to be ready to serve their country.”

     The lesson of the Long March is not just about history. It is about the spirit that every Chinese
person should carry in their heart. In a fast-changing world, challenges will always come. But if we
remember the courage of those who walked the Long March, we will have the strength to face any
difficulty. As one saying goes, “The Long March is over, but the spirit of the Long March never
ends.”

【阅读原文】

      The year 2026 marks the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Long March. To remember this
extraordinary history, thirty young people from Macao traveled to Ruijin in Jiangxi, the starting

point of the Long March. They retraced the old Red Army routes and visited historic revolutionary
sites, getting a real, impressive lesson about the extraordinary years.

      The Long March took place between 1934 and 1936. Over 86,000 Red Army soldiers set out to
break through enemy blocks and escape continuous pursuit. They climbed 18 snow-covered
mountains, crossed 24 rivers and struggled through deadly swamps. When they finally reached
northern Shaanxi safely, more than 40,000 soldiers had sacrificed their lives for the revolutionary

learn this history in fresh ways, including watching 5D movies and joining immersive “living-
history walks” to experience soldiers’ tough life personally.

      The Long March was filled with suffering, yet it was also full of hope. Its spirit teaches
teenagers to hold fast to faith and never give up when meeting troubles in daily life, and this
precious spirit will always go on from generation to generation.

Passage 3     长征 90 年：我们不曾忘记

词数：352       用时：8 分钟        蓝思值：750L-850L

【阅读原文】

      The year 2026 marks the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Long March. To remember this
great event, a group of 30 young people from Macao traveled to Ruijin, Jiangxi—the starting point
of the Long March. They walked the old Red Army paths and visited the historic sites. For many of
them, this trip was a first-time, eye-opening experience.

      The Long March took place from 1934 to 1936. The Red Army had to break through enemy
lines and march across China to escape the enemy’s pursuit. More than 86,000 soldiers started the
journey. They climbed 18 snow mountains and crossed 24 rivers. They also walked through
dangerous swamps (沼泽). By the time they reached safety, over 40,000 soldiers had lost their lives
for the revolutionary cause.

      How did the Red Army make it against all odds? The answer lies in their strong will. They
carried heavy loads —guns, blankets, and cooking pots—on their backs. They slept in the open air
and ate wild plants. They faced hunger and cold, but they never gave up. Historians believe that the
soldiers’ strong discipline (纪律) and their ability to stay united were the keys to their survival.

      Today, every year over 600,000 people visit the Long March memorial (纪念的) sites. Young
people learn about the Long March in new ways. Some watch 5D movies about it. Some take part in
“living-history walks,” where they sleep on the ground and eat simple meals just like the Red Army
did. These activities help them better understand the hardship the Red Army faced.

      What can the Long March teach us today? A local guide said, “The Long March was full of
suffering, but it was also full of hope. It tells young people, no matter how hard life gets, if you

                                                    4
keep trying and never give up, you can get through it.” The spirit of the Long March lives on,
encouraging new generations to face the “mountains and rivers” in their own lives.
